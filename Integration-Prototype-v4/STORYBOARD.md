# A presence that gradually takes up more space

## Interface audit

The previous version put a six-stage overview, technical mode copy, permission explanations, media controls, answer fields, AI outputs, evidence and feedback in large stacks. The repeated bordered cards and permanently visible controls made the participant scan a form instead of following a conversation. The previous ending switched to a development-status panel just when the participant's judgement should have mattered most. These are presentation problems, not reasons to discard the working camera, recording, identity logic, service adapters or data controls.

## Visual direction

- Typography: Palatino-style serif for invitations and participant words; compact monospaced lettering for AI labels, progress, evidence and processing. One question or revelation dominates each scene.
- Colour: warm paper and green supplied-data labels at first; a muted lilac machine accent at inference; dusty pink at prediction; dark ink and stronger pink for the double and fictional scene. Labels always include words and symbols, not colour alone.
- Space: Stage 1 image and Stage 2 voice or words occupy the widest area. Stage 4 deliberately gives prediction and actual choice equal panels. Stage 5 introduces the participant/double split only when the double exists. Stage 6 gives the invented scene one reading surface.
- Imagery: only participant-supplied image and separately captured portrait. No stock illustration. The camera dock appears only when a consented camera stream is live.
- Motion: a short question reveal and quick scene entrance. No mandatory animation or sound. Reduced-motion settings reveal text immediately. The progress line remains small.
- Controls: one next action near the current moment; Back, Skip, View my data, Delete session and Pause / leave remain accessible. Evidence and secondary media choices are closed until requested.

## Storyboard

| Moment | Initially visible | After interaction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | Project question, approximate duration, two short cautions, Begin. | Optional “Before you begin” expands to data, AI and skip details; Begin moves to the image. |
| 1. I SEE YOU | Invitation and equal upload/camera choices. | Mirrored camera becomes the focus only after permission; capture shows the chosen image, source label, replace/delete and confirmation. The image stays on screen before continuing. |
| 2. I LISTEN TO YOU | One large question and equal Speak/Type choices. | Speak shows record status, timer, playback, separate transcription consent and editable words. Type goes straight to a quiet writing surface. Neither route is a hidden fallback. |
| 3. I THINK I KNOW YOU | One existing question and Speak/Type, never all three at once. | The participant's answer appears first, then one tentative machine interpretation. Accept/Correct/Reject are inline. Evidence is under “Why did the AI say this?”; after question three, a compact profile summary appears. |
| 4. I CAN PREDICT YOU | Existing dilemma and “Hear its prediction”; no answer field. | A labelled prediction appears first; only then can the participant speak or type. “Place our answers together” reveals equal-sized prediction and actual-choice panels, then asks how close it was. |
| 5. I CAN BE YOU | Existing proxy situation and separate permission for the system to answer. | Optional voice and portrait choices are exposed without taking over the scene. A generated response creates the participant/double split. Actual video, still/audio or text-only fallback is named honestly; review and correction follow. |
| 6. I DON’T NEED YOU | Plain warning that the next scene is fictional and a generation choice. | The scene is prominent but retains its fictional warning. A small ledger distinguishes borrowed fragments, an earlier inference and concrete inventions. The final question appears only after the scene. |
| Ending | The participant's Yes/Partly/No/Unsure judgement leads. | They can inspect separated session data, leave optional feedback or prominently delete the session. No development-status dashboard appears. |

## Reliability notes

Permission denial keeps upload or typing available. A failed transcription leaves the recording playable and offers retry or manual wording; in Stage 3 the participant can also continue with the saved recording without it being called skipped, while the AI explicitly declines to interpret unavailable words. OpenAI failure keeps earlier answers and offers clearly simulated output. Voice or D-ID failure leaves the generated text and any completed audio or still portrait, with the missing medium explicitly named. All service loading and recovery messages use the existing live-status system and do not advance the scene automatically.
